## Les shopping list

https://a3-hawthornwood.onrender.com/

Basically my project allows people to log in and keep a shopping list personalized to their username. Its pretty simple but it gets the job done. The goal is to keep your shopping items in an easy place. Challanges I faced were... pretty much every step of the process. I had a lot of trouble figuring out how to setup mongodb and how it works especially, since I was already grappling with learning js. That being said I understand a lot more of mongodb and express and js so thats a win. I initially started making the authentication use a seperate collection for users which it'll refer to, then I realized its late at night the day this is due, so I pivoted to having each insertion of data have a field called userpass which stores the username and password. Then, when you log in it saves your username and password, and it only gets/modifies/deletes items with your username and password. Specifically, it stores it like this "user##@password". I used picnic https://picnicss.com/documentation because it looked clean and fit the clean vibe of a shopping list. I used modifications in the form of a different css sheet that goes after it, which sets some nice colors, centers things, and just makes it all fit better.

### Design/Evaluation Achievements
- For the CRAP priciples, the text boxes recieve the most contrast, as they glow when you hover over them. This is important since they're the most important part of the website. Additionally, the center div which contains all of the information, despite being light blue like the background, is heavily contrasted with the background. I did this by 1. having the center div have a more saturated shade of blue than the background, and 2. highlighting the center div in a ring of orange, brown, and black. It's also sandwhiched by the dark blue header and footer, which increases it's contrast by centering it as a wreath around it, the same was the orange brown and black ring does it. I also made the text black when the background it lighter, and the text white when the background is darker, which makes it more readable and thus more accessable to people with eye issues. DEI win!
  - I used proximity to group the inputs into one section and the responses (the shopping list) into another section, by putting the elements in each group close together like a list. To elaborate (quoth the 125) all of the inputs are groups together in a blocky format. Additionally, there is texts sandwhiched inbetween the inputs. This tells the person operating the website that the text must be related to the input. So, due to proximity people know that text is giving instructions for what to write in the input. Like how right under "Remove data" theres a block touching it (no other blocks are that close) which is used for writing the piece of data you want to remove. And then of course, all of the entries for you user's shopping list and listed in an actual list, which is a close proximity thing that shows its a group of something. 
  - I used solid color backgrounds for divs repeatedly, so it all blends together as a vibrant but not focused background. Also, as mentioned earlier, I spammed the hover affect for the inputs. Like, yeah it works well for the contrast. You know what it's also? cool. And personally thats what I think the most important part of this is, rule of cool. I also used google's grandstander font for pretty much all of the font in the website. My reasoning is that none of the text is different enough to justify having a different font. Maybe if I had a piece of joke text I'd use a wacky font for that, but alas, all my text is normal and not wacky. Take a look at my A1...
  - I used alignment by keeping headers centered and non-headers to the left so they read more like regular text. I also tried to center all of the major divs so that they are centered to where someone would naturally be looking at the screen. Speaking of divs, the css library randomly added a bit of space into one of my center most divs and its really annoying!!! Anyhow yeah in addition to centering headers I also centered the text in the real header and footer. This is because they kinda add onto the background like the tops and bottoms of roman columns, so I kinda have to center them or it doesn't feel right. And then everything thats to the left is like that because it contains or will be typed with text that people are used to seeing on the left.
